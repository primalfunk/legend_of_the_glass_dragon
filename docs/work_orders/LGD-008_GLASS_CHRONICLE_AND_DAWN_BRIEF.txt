LGD-008 — GLASS CHRONICLE + DAWN BRIEF

STATUS

AUTHORIZED FOR IMPLEMENTATION

MISSION

Complete the game's day-transition experience so that ending a day feels like
watching a small fantasy society continue living after the player goes to bed.

Legend of the Glass Dragon is not merely an offline recreation of Legend of the
Red Dragon. Its central twist is:

* the player may play as many consecutive days as desired;
* the world continues to act between those days;
* recurring simulated residents pursue their own lives;
* events have consequences;
* the player can watch history accumulating around them.

LGD-008 should make that promise visible through this polished transition:

END DAY
→ WORLD RESOLUTION
→ THE GLASS CHRONICLE
→ DAWN BRIEF
→ NEXT PLAYABLE DAY

The Chronicle and Dawn Brief serve different purposes and must remain distinct.

======================================================================
1. THE CORE DISTINCTION
======================================================================

THE GLASS CHRONICLE represents what the GAME knows. It may show important events
regardless of whether the player's character witnessed them or could reasonably
have learned about them. It is the player's privileged view into the simulated
world.

Chronicle examples include defeats deep in the forest, NPC fights, levels,
important equipment, rivalries, disappearances, wealth changes, challenges,
near-death escapes, quiet preparation, and meaningful ranking changes.

THE DAWN BRIEF represents what the CHARACTER knows when the next day begins. It
contains only information plausibly available through direct consequences,
public knowledge, visible world changes, gossip, notices, rankings, community
information, messages, and consequences affecting the player.

The Dawn Brief must not duplicate the Chronicle.

======================================================================
2. WHY THIS MATTERS
======================================================================

The system should create recognizable, continuing community stories: residents
fight while the player sleeps, old rivals improve, equipment changes hands,
adventurers disappear or return wounded, and rankings change. Existing
simulation changes must be exposed coherently instead of remaining silent state.

======================================================================
3. END DAY FLOW
======================================================================

When the player ends the day:

1. Commit the player's completed-day state safely.
2. Resolve all authorized NPC/world simulation for the transition.
3. Capture meaningful transition events as structured facts.
4. Derive the Glass Chronicle from those facts.
5. Derive the Dawn Brief from the same authoritative resulting world state.
6. Present them in sequence.
7. Begin the next playable day only after the presentation is completed or
   intentionally skipped.

Simulation produces state and events. Presentation interprets them. Presentation
must not become authoritative simulation.

======================================================================
4. TRANSITION EVENT RECORD
======================================================================

Create or refine a structured transition-event representation. It may include:

* event type;
* involved resident IDs;
* location;
* before/after values;
* public/private visibility;
* importance;
* causal relationship;
* related prior incident/story;
* resulting relationship change;
* whether it directly affects the player;
* optional narrative parameters.

Do not store finished English prose as the canonical event model where structured
facts are more appropriate. Narrative text derives from authoritative facts.

======================================================================
5. EVENT IMPORTANCE
======================================================================

Do not report routine low-value simulation noise. Use a practical importance
system. High importance includes permanent loss where supported, major defeat or
victory, level advancement, rare equipment, major rank changes, formal duels,
meaningful relationship changes, major reversals, factions when available, and
Dragon events when available. Medium importance includes recurring rivalry,
surprising defeat, exceptional profit, serious escape, story callbacks, and
entering/leaving meaningful states. Usually omit low importance.

The Chronicle is curated, not a debug log.

======================================================================
6. CHRONICLE LENGTH
======================================================================

Target roughly 3–7 Chronicle items on an active day, fewer when little happened,
and occasionally more only for an unusually consequential transition. Do not
fabricate filler. Prioritize interest and combine tightly related actions where
useful.

======================================================================
7. THE GLASS CHRONICLE PRESENTATION
======================================================================

Create a dedicated ANSI-style old-BBS news/history presentation using LGD-007's
visual language. Suggested heading:

THE GLASS CHRONICLE
Day <N> passes into history...

Items should be concise, narrative, and text-first. Do not use feed cards,
social-media presentation, or giant article pages.

======================================================================
8. NARRATIVE VARIETY
======================================================================

Provide bounded deterministic variation for common event families. Variation
must not alter facts. Select templates deterministically from event/state data.

======================================================================
9. CONTINUITY AND CALLBACKS
======================================================================

Prefer references to real history—prior insults, repeated victories, established
feuds, memories, incidents, and relationship state—where supporting data exists.
Do not invent continuity absent from game state.

======================================================================
10. PLAYER VISIBILITY
======================================================================

Distinguish:

PLAYER KNOWS BECAUSE THEY WITNESSED IT
PLAYER LEARNS PUBLICLY AT DAWN
PLAYER DOES NOT KNOW, BUT THE CHRONICLE SHOWS THE HUMAN PLAYER

Encode visibility explicitly where practical.

======================================================================
11. DAWN BRIEF CONTENT
======================================================================

After the Chronicle, present a shorter, actionable:

DAWN BRIEF
Day <N+1>

It may include player health/status, daily resources, overnight gold/bank
changes, directed messages/challenges, obvious public rankings, public
deaths/disappearances, notices, knowable relationship developments, major
economy/shop changes, and other direct consequences. It answers what the
adventurer needs to know before playing today.

======================================================================
12. INFORMATION BOUNDARY
======================================================================

A secret forest fight may appear with full participants in the Chronicle while
the Dawn Brief reveals only a public consequence such as an adventurer returning
badly wounded. Preserve this philosophical distinction.

======================================================================
13. SKIP / ADVANCE BEHAVIOR
======================================================================

Allow normal reading, next-screen advance, skipping remaining Chronicle
presentation, and quick entry into the new day. Skip changes presentation only;
world resolution still occurs exactly once.

======================================================================
14. CHRONICLE HISTORY
======================================================================

Persist a bounded historical record in save state. It should support future
season chronicles, callbacks, retrospectives, and endgame narration without
unbounded save growth. Retaining important facts for one finite season is
preferred where storage cost is modest.

======================================================================
15. DAILY NEWS / EXISTING SYSTEM CONSOLIDATION
======================================================================

Inventory and consolidate Daily News, Morning Report, ranking notices, Inn
gossip, incident feeds, and simulation result presentation. Recommended authority:

GLASS CHRONICLE = omniscient historical recap.
DAWN BRIEF = player-actionable/newly knowable information.
INN GOSSIP = incomplete, colorful local social information.
RANKINGS = authoritative current standings.

Refactor or replace overlapping systems instead of creating permanent duplicates.

======================================================================
16. PLAYER-CREATED HISTORY
======================================================================

Chronicle important player events too: levels, rival victories, notable defeats,
major acquisitions, exceptional standing, and significant story events. It is a
realm history that includes the player, not merely a report about bots.

======================================================================
17. DAY NUMBER AND TIME
======================================================================

Make explicit which day ended, which transition is being recorded, and which day
begins. Use game-day language rather than real dates.

======================================================================
18. DETERMINISM
======================================================================

Identical state, RNG seed/state, and player actions must produce the same world
outcome, selected events, narrative template choices, and Dawn Brief. Narrative
variation must not perturb gameplay RNG.

======================================================================
19. SAVE / RESTORE
======================================================================

The transition must survive closing immediately after End Day, during Chronicle,
between Chronicle and Dawn, on Dawn, and after Android process recreation. The day
must never resolve twice or duplicate simulation, money, items, or advancement.
Persist enough phase/state to reconstruct presentation safely.

======================================================================
20. LONG-RUN EXPERIENCE
======================================================================

Inspect early days, several consecutive transitions, approximately Day 10, and a
longer accelerated run. Watch for repetitive language, resident domination,
noise, implausible counts, contradictions, stale incidents, knowledge leaks, and
unexplained relationship changes.

======================================================================
21. VISUAL REQUIREMENTS
======================================================================

Use LGD-007's ANSI language. Chronicle uses black, terminal framing, cyan/white,
gold for notable positive/world events, red/magenta for danger, and restrained
ornament. Dawn is related but cleaner and optimized for scanning.

======================================================================
22. AUDIO
======================================================================

Music continues through transition. Valid Advance/Continue/Skip selections use
the existing click. Add no fanfare, dawn music, page turn, narration, or event
sounds.

======================================================================
23. OUT OF SCOPE
======================================================================

Do not implement the Glass Dragon boss, Dragon Season pressure, factions, new
combat, major economy redesign, Main Menu, more sounds, large populations,
cutscenes, or achievements.

======================================================================
24. TESTING
======================================================================

Targeted tests must prove:

1. End Day resolves exactly once.
2. Chronicle generation reads resulting transition events.
3. Low-value noise is filtered.
4. Significant events are retained.
5. Ordering is deterministic.
6. Narrative variation is deterministic.
7. Dawn excludes private information.
8. Direct player consequences appear.
9. Skip does not alter simulation.
10. Chronicle resume does not rerun End Day.
11. Dawn resume does not rerun End Day.
12. History persists.
13. Old records remain immutable.
14. Repeated days remain deterministic.
15. Existing saves migrate safely.

======================================================================
25. PLAYER-FACING QUALIFICATION
======================================================================

Perform a real multi-day playthrough and judge whether residents matter,
yesterday affects tomorrow, recurring names and rivalries are recognizable,
Chronicle surprises, Dawn is useful, skip is quick, and End Day creates curiosity.
If it reads like a simulation log, adjust it.

======================================================================
26. COMPLETION STANDARD
======================================================================

LGD-008 is complete when a player can finish a day, End Day, advance the world
exactly once, read a concise curated Chronicle, recognize continuity, advance to
a distinct knowable/actionable Dawn Brief, begin the next day, and repeat without
corruption, duplication, or severe narrative repetition.

The central success criterion is emotional: ENDING THE DAY SHOULD BE INTERESTING.

======================================================================

======================================================================

Return disposition, implementation commit/HEAD, transition architecture, event
model, Chronicle selection and visibility rules, Dawn rules, consolidation,
persistence/migration, automated results, multi-day play qualification, actual
examples, genuine defects, and whether LGD-009 may proceed.
