LEGEND OF THE GLASS DRAGON
WORK ORDER LGD-005
SHOPS, BANK, EXPANDED INN, EQUIPMENT ECONOMY, AND ADVENTURER CHALLENGES

STATUS

AUTHORIZED FOR IMPLEMENTATION


GOVERNING BASELINE

LGD-001 established the native Android / Kotlin / Jetpack Compose foundation and ANSI/BBS presentation.

LGD-002 established:

- character creation;
- persistent player identity;
- Continue Game;
- GameState persistence;
- Town;
- Character Status.

LGD-003 established:

- Forest combat;
- monster encounters;
- HP and healing;
- experience and gold;
- training and leveling;
- finite daily fights;
- defeat/recovery;
- consecutive game-day advancement.

LGD-004 established:

- a persistent simulated adventurer population;
- daily NPC activity;
- NPC progression;
- NPC equipment improvement;
- Rankings;
- Realm News;
- morning reports;
- Inn community presence;
- gossip;
- deterministic world advancement.

LGD-005 should expand the existing playable loop rather than introduce a new architectural direction.


PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Turn the Village from a collection of functional destinations into a meaningful BBS RPG economy and social competition hub.

At completion, the player must be able to:

- buy and upgrade weapons;
- buy and upgrade armor;
- deposit gold safely;
- withdraw gold;
- understand the difference between carried and banked wealth;
- interact more meaningfully with the simulated adventurers in the Inn;
- challenge another adventurer;
- win or lose those encounters;
- create rivalry, news, and ranking consequences from those encounters;
- see player and NPC equipment participate in one coherent economy/progression system.

This should make gold, equipment, rankings, and rival adventurers matter substantially more than they do at the LGD-004 baseline.


PRODUCT FEEL

This should feel like exploring additional doors on a busy BBS game menu.

For example:

    -=- YE OLDE WEAPON SHOPPE -=-

    You carry: 327 gold

    [1] Short Sword........ 100
    [2] Broad Sword........ 250
    [3] Steel Longsword.... 500

    Your weapon:
    Rusty Sword

    [4] Sell Current Weapon
    [5] Return to Town

The player taps lines.

Do not turn shops into:

- image grids;
- shopping cards;
- item icons;
- modern store tabs;
- graphical currency badges.

The game remains text-first.


PART 1 — UNIFY THE EQUIPMENT CATALOG

LGD-004 introduced lightweight NPC equipment progression.

Formalize the equipment model so player and NPC characters refer to the same canonical catalog where appropriate.

At minimum support:

WeaponDefinition

- stable ID;
- display name;
- attack contribution;
- purchase price;
- resale value or resale rule;
- progression tier;
- minimum level if required.

ArmorDefinition

- stable ID;
- display name;
- defense contribution;
- purchase price;
- resale value or resale rule;
- progression tier;
- minimum level if required.

Do not store complete duplicated item statistics separately inside every character save if stable catalog IDs are sufficient.

The save should preserve item identity lawfully across reloads.


PART 2 — EQUIPMENT LADDER

Create a modest initial progression ladder.

Do not create dozens of items.

A useful first pass might contain approximately:

- 6–10 weapons;
- 6–10 armor pieces.

The early tiers should be realistically affordable during ordinary play.

Later tiers should provide goals over multiple game days.

Example tone:

WEAPONS

- Rusty Sword
- Short Sword
- Broad Sword
- Steel Longsword
- Battle Axe
- Runed Blade

ARMOR

- Worn Leather
- Leather Armor
- Studded Leather
- Chain Mail
- Scale Armor
- Plate Mail

Exact names and numbers are Builder discretion.

Maintain the light reskin / Glass Dragon identity rather than reproducing an old game's item list verbatim if there is a better thematic option.


PART 3 — PLAYER COMBAT STATS

Ensure attack and defense derive coherently from:

- base character growth;
- equipment contribution;
- any already-existing stat fields.

Avoid accidentally counting equipment twice.

If LGD-003 stored final Attack and Defense directly and LGD-005 now needs base/equipment separation, migrate the model deliberately.

The UI should remain able to display simple understandable totals such as:

    Attack...... 11
    Defense..... 8

Optional detail may appear in Character Status:

    Attack...... 11  (6 + 5 weapon)
    Defense.....  8  (4 + 4 armor)

Do not expose unnecessary internal formulas.


PART 4 — WEAPON SHOP

Replace any placeholder with a functional weapon merchant.

At minimum support:

- current weapon display;
- current carried gold;
- available weapons;
- price;
- basic attack improvement;
- purchase;
- sale/trade-in handling;
- refusal when unaffordable;
- refusal when level-gated if level gates are used;
- return to Town.

Purchases must be atomic.

Rapid taps must not:

- buy twice;
- deduct gold twice;
- duplicate items.

The player should not need to manually manage an inventory of obsolete weapons unless that adds clear value.

Preferred initial rule:

The player owns one equipped weapon.

Buying another weapon replaces the current one, with a documented resale/trade-in value applied if appropriate.


PART 5 — ARMOR SHOP

Implement armor purchasing using the same general principles as the Weapon Shop.

At minimum:

- current armor;
- carried gold;
- available armor;
- defense effect;
- purchase;
- trade-in/resale;
- affordability handling.

Avoid duplicating shop logic unnecessarily where a shared shop/economy service is appropriate.

Do not force both shops into one generic abstraction if doing so makes the code harder to understand.


PART 6 — PURCHASE EXPERIENCE

Buying something should produce a small BBS-style acknowledgement.

Example:

    The smith takes your gold.

    You are now armed with:
    Steel Longsword

    Attack increased to 14!

    [1] Continue Shopping
    [2] Return to Town

Keep feedback concise.

Do not animate an equipment panel or open a modern toast for major purchases.


PART 7 — ECONOMY RULES

Centralize core economy rules.

At minimum define:

- equipment price;
- resale/trade-in value;
- healer cost;
- monster gold rewards;
- death gold loss;
- bank deposit behavior;
- bank withdrawal behavior.

The economy should be internally coherent enough that:

- Forest victories produce meaningful purchasing power;
- better equipment requires some saving;
- defeat makes carried gold meaningfully risky;
- the bank provides a reason to visit Town.

Do not attempt final economy balance yet.


PART 8 — BANK

Implement a functional Bank.

Add persistent:

- carried gold;
- banked gold.

The player should be able to:

- deposit gold;
- withdraw gold;
- deposit all;
- withdraw all;
- return to Town.

A terminal-style flow might be:

    -=- FIRST BANK OF THE REALM -=-

    On hand:  327 gold
    In bank:  850 gold

    [1] Deposit Gold
    [2] Withdraw Gold
    [3] Deposit All
    [4] Withdraw All
    [5] Return to Town


PART 9 — GOLD ENTRY

Do not require free-form keyboard entry for ordinary banking unless it clearly improves usability.

Prefer touch-driven amount choices.

Possible design:

    How much do you wish to deposit?

    [1] 25 gold
    [2] 50 gold
    [3] 100 gold
    [4] Half
    [5] All
    [6] Cancel

Another acceptable solution is a terminal-styled numeric selector if convenient.

The project principle remains:

TEXT ENTRY ONLY WHERE IT IS GENUINELY BETTER.

Do not summon the software keyboard merely because historical BBS software expected typed numbers.


PART 10 — BANK SAFETY

Banked gold must be protected from ordinary Forest defeat.

Only carried gold should participate in the LGD-003 defeat loss unless a future explicit mechanic changes that.

Verify:

- carried gold never goes negative;
- banked gold never goes negative;
- deposits cannot exceed carried gold;
- withdrawals cannot exceed banked gold;
- a transaction executes exactly once;
- save/load preserves both balances.


PART 11 — PLAYER EQUIPMENT AND NPC ECONOMY COHERENCE

NPC equipment upgrades from LGD-004 should now use the canonical weapon/armor catalogs and real purchase prices where practical.

NPCs should not simply materialize equipment for free.

Their lightweight economy should account for:

- available gold;
- desired upgrade;
- actual equipment cost.

It is acceptable for NPC purchase logic to remain abstract and outcome-oriented.

Do not force NPCs to visit UI shop screens.

The important rule is that NPC wealth and equipment should broadly obey the same world economy.


PART 12 — NPC EQUIPMENT VISIBILITY

NPC profiles should now show meaningful equipment.

Example:

    -=- MIRA -=-

    Rank......... 3
    Level........ 5
    Weapon....... Broad Sword
    Armor........ Chain Mail

    Recent News:
    Defeated a Forest Troll on Day 12.

    [1] Challenge Mira
    [2] Return

This should help players form opinions such as:

"Mira has better armor than I do."

That comparison is desirable.


PART 13 — EXPANDED INN

Expand the Inn from an information location into the first meaningful social/action hub.

Suggested structure:

    -=- THE DRAGON'S REST -=-

    [1] Hear Gossip
    [2] Read Realm News
    [3] See Who Is Here
    [4] Talk to an Adventurer
    [5] Challenge an Adventurer
    [6] Return to Town

"Talk" may remain modest in this phase.

The important new interaction is:

CHALLENGE AN ADVENTURER.


PART 14 — WHO IS AT THE INN

Each game day, determine a bounded subset of NPC adventurers who are present at the Inn.

Presence should be:

- persistent for that game day;
- derived deterministically from the world state / controlled randomness;
- influenced by status/personality if convenient.

A recovering adventurer might plausibly be more likely to appear.

An adventurer currently incapacitated by another state should not appear inconsistently.

The player should not need access to every NPC from every Inn menu every day.


PART 15 — ADVENTURER CHALLENGES

Implement player-vs-simulated-adventurer combat.

This is NOT real multiplayer.

The player challenges a locally simulated persistent NPC.

Use the existing combat engine/rules where practical.

The opponent should derive combat values from their actual:

- level;
- stats;
- weapon;
- armor.

Do not create a separate arbitrary "PvP power score" if the existing rules can cleanly support the encounter.


PART 16 — CHALLENGE ELIGIBILITY

Not every NPC should necessarily be challengeable at all times.

At minimum prevent challenges when:

- NPC is not in an eligible active state;
- player is already defeated for the day;
- another challenge is currently resolving.

Optional guardrails may include:

- level-difference warnings;
- daily challenge limits.

Do not overcomplicate eligibility.

The system should principally prevent incoherent or exploitative states.


PART 17 — DAILY PLAYER CHALLENGE LIMIT

Introduce a small daily limit for adventurer challenges.

Recommended initial rule:

    1 challenge per game day

or:

    2 challenges per game day

Builder may choose based on play feel.

This creates consequence and prevents the Inn from becoming an unlimited gold/XP farming loop.

Display the remaining opportunity clearly.

Example:

    Adventurer Challenge Remaining: 1


PART 18 — CHALLENGE ACCEPTANCE

For LGD-005, NPC challenge acceptance may be simple.

Possible rule:

- most eligible NPCs accept;
- cautious personalities may occasionally refuse;
- aggressive personalities almost always accept;
- NPCs significantly stronger/weaker may react differently.

A refusal should produce flavor, not break the interaction.

Example:

    Cedric laughs.

    "Not today. Find someone else to bleed on."

Do not build a negotiation system.


PART 19 — ADVENTURER COMBAT PRESENTATION

Distinguish an adventurer duel from a monster encounter while reusing core mechanics.

Example:

    -=- A CHALLENGE! -=-

    Arcturus
    Level 5
    HP: 41 / 41

           versus

    Mira
    Level 5
    HP: 39 / 39

    [1] Attack
    [2] Yield

Use "Yield" rather than "Run Away" if it better fits the duel.

The combat log should identify the opponent by name.

Example:

    You strike Mira for 7 damage!

    Mira answers with a vicious blow for 5!


PART 20 — PLAYER VICTORY IN A CHALLENGE

A victory should matter, but should not destroy NPC continuity.

On player victory:

- resolve opponent defeat lawfully;
- record the challenge result;
- generate a WorldEvent;
- modify familiarity/rivalry;
- apply a modest reward if appropriate;
- update relevant statistics.

Potential reward options:

- small gold stake;
- small experience award;
- prestige/ranking contribution.

Do NOT award the same rewards as a full Forest monster grind if that creates easy farming.

The exact initial reward is Builder discretion.


PART 21 — PLAYER LOSS / YIELD

If the player loses:

- the save remains lawful;
- apply a meaningful but recoverable consequence;
- generate community news where notable;
- update relationship/rivalry;
- consume the daily challenge opportunity.

Prefer not to end the entire player's Forest fighting day automatically unless playtesting shows that is desirable.

A duel loss and a Forest near-death do not have to be identical.

If the player chooses Yield:

- combat ends;
- no victory reward;
- challenge is consumed;
- rivalry/familiarity may change modestly.

Do not permit Yield after the opponent is already defeated.


PART 22 — NPC CONSEQUENCE OF PLAYER DUEL

A defeated NPC should remain the same persistent NPC.

Potential consequence:

- temporary RECOVERING status;
- modest carried gold loss if such a model exists;
- daily action reduction;
- rivalry increase.

Be cautious about allowing the player to repeatedly cripple the entire simulated population.

A challenge should create history, not permanently sabotage the simulation.


PART 23 — NPC-VS-NPC CHALLENGES

Extend world simulation with a lightweight possibility of adventurers challenging one another.

Do not simulate these through full combat turns.

Resolve them using outcome-oriented logic.

Generate notable events such as:

    Raven defeated Thorn in a challenge.

    Mira embarrassed Cedric at the Inn.

    Selene has challenged Raven for bragging rights.

This should reinforce the sense that the community has social competition independent of the player.


PART 24 — RIVALRY V1

Expand the LGD-004 familiarity foundation with a simple rivalry value or relationship classification.

Do not build a giant relationship engine.

At minimum, repeated events such as:

- player challenging NPC;
- NPC beating player;
- player beating NPC;
- overtaking each other in rankings;

may increase rivalry.

The UI may expose only a readable label such as:

    Attitude..... Rival

or:

    You and Mira have become fierce rivals.

Do not display raw numbers unless useful for debugging.


PART 25 — REMEMBERED CHALLENGE HISTORY

NPCs should not behave as if a duel never happened.

Store enough compact history to support later references.

At minimum consider:

- player wins vs this NPC;
- player losses vs this NPC;
- most recent challenge day;
- rivalry/familiarity.

This can drive gossip such as:

    "Mira is still angry about the beating
     you gave her yesterday."

or:

    "Raven says you won't get lucky twice."


PART 26 — REALM NEWS INTEGRATION

Generate news from meaningful player/NPC challenge outcomes.

Examples:

    Arcturus defeated Mira at the Inn!

    Raven bested Thorn in a challenge.

    Cedric forced Arcturus to yield.

Prioritize noteworthy events.

Do not generate a giant transcript of every attack.


PART 27 — GOSSIP INTEGRATION

Update gossip generation to reference:

- recent player challenges;
- known rivalries;
- equipment purchases;
- bank/economy only where publicly plausible;
- rank changes.

Do not have NPCs mysteriously know the exact value of the player's private bank account.

World information should remain plausible within the BBS fiction.


PART 28 — RANKINGS AND CHALLENGES

Do not automatically change the primary level/XP ranking simply because someone wins one duel unless that follows the existing progression system.

However, challenge outcomes may:

- affect XP modestly;
- create a separate win/loss indicator;
- influence descriptive prestige.

If adding an explicit PvP record, keep it simple.

Example NPC profile:

    Challenges... 4 wins / 2 losses

A separate "greatest fighter" leaderboard is optional and should only be added if it remains visually simple and provides clear value.


PART 29 — PLAYER CHARACTER STATUS EXPANSION

Update Character Status to include:

- carried gold;
- banked gold;
- weapon;
- armor;
- current attack/defense;
- challenge record if implemented;
- current rank.

Keep it compact enough to feel like an old character record screen.


PART 30 — TOWN MENU EXPANSION

By the end of LGD-005, Town should include functional access to approximately:

    [1] Enter the Forest
    [2] Visit the Healer
    [3] Weapon Shop
    [4] Armor Shop
    [5] Bank
    [6] Training Hall
    [7] The Inn
    [8] Rankings
    [9] Character
    [0] End the Day

Exact numbering/layout may differ.

If the menu becomes too tall for some devices:

- use scrolling;
- maintain generous touch targets;
- do not shrink text into illegibility.

A classic numbered menu may happily scroll on a modern touchscreen.


PART 31 — ECONOMY BALANCE PASS

Run a practical early-game balance pass.

A normal player should be able to:

- afford their first meaningful upgrade within a reasonable number of fights/days;
- understand why banking gold is useful;
- feel some tension about carrying a large amount into the Forest;
- save toward clearly stronger equipment;
- avoid becoming instantly rich from NPC challenges.

NPCs should also upgrade over time without all purchasing the same tier on the same day.


PART 32 — COMBAT BALANCE WITH EQUIPMENT

Verify that equipment now produces meaningful combat differences.

Expected:

- buying a better weapon noticeably improves offensive performance;
- buying armor noticeably improves survival;
- equipment advantages are meaningful but not absolute;
- an under-equipped character can still occasionally beat a similar-level rival;
- a vastly stronger opponent remains dangerous.

Do not overfit final balance.


PART 33 — SAVE SCHEMA EVOLUTION

LGD-005 materially expands GameState.

Handle:

- banked gold;
- canonical equipment IDs;
- challenge counts;
- rivalry/history;
- Inn daily presence if persisted;
- any new NPC economy state.

Use the existing schema/version strategy.

Migrate lawful LGD-004 saves where reasonably simple.

If development-era reset remains the project policy, make incompatibility explicit rather than silently corrupting state.


PART 34 — ATOMIC ACTION SAFETY

High-value actions now include:

- buying equipment;
- selling/trading equipment;
- bank deposit;
- bank withdrawal;
- beginning a challenge;
- resolving a challenge reward;
- End Day.

Rapid tapping and lifecycle events must not:

- duplicate purchases;
- duplicate sale credit;
- duplicate withdrawals;
- create gold;
- duplicate duel rewards;
- consume multiple daily challenges.

Treat these operations as logical atomic transitions in the game layer.


PART 35 — TEST REQUIREMENTS

Add meaningful automated tests for:

EQUIPMENT

- catalog IDs unique;
- lawful prices;
- stronger tiers improve appropriate stat;
- equipment lookup survives save/load;
- purchase deducts exactly once;
- trade-in/resale correct;
- unaffordable purchase rejected;
- duplicate tap cannot duplicate acquisition.

COMBAT STATS

- base + equipment calculation correct;
- equipment not double-counted;
- stat values remain lawful.

BANK

- deposit;
- withdrawal;
- deposit all;
- withdraw all;
- insufficient funds;
- carried/banked gold never negative;
- banked gold protected from ordinary Forest defeat;
- save/load round trip.

NPC ECONOMY

- NPC cannot buy unaffordable equipment;
- purchase reduces gold;
- NPC uses canonical equipment catalog;
- progression remains lawful.

CHALLENGES

- eligible opponent can be challenged;
- ineligible opponent rejected;
- daily challenge limit enforced;
- player victory;
- player loss;
- yield;
- reward exactly once;
- challenge opportunity consumed exactly once;
- NPC continuity preserved;
- relationship/rivalry updated.

NPC-VS-NPC

- bounded simulation;
- lawful outcome;
- news event generated where appropriate.

NEWS/GOSSIP

- player challenge result may appear in Realm News;
- rivalry can influence gossip;
- private bank balance is not leaked by generic gossip.

LONG RUN

Extend the deterministic simulation soak test so at least 1,000 days including:

- NPC equipment purchases;
- NPC challenge activity;
- economy changes;

remain structurally lawful.

NEXT PLANNED WORK

LGD-006 — Special Forest Events, Richer Inn Encounters, Relationships, and Persistent Community Stories

The next work should focus less on adding another pile of economic mechanics and more on making consecutive days memorable.

The goal will be to turn the existing systems into stories:

- unexpected Forest discoveries;
- recurring encounters with named adventurers;
- friendships and rivalries that develop visibly;
- gossip that remembers what happened;
- rare community incidents;
- small choices with consequences across later game days.

By the end of that phase, the simulated BBS community should begin feeling less like a leaderboard full of competent bots and more like a strange little town the player has come to know.
