LEGEND OF THE GLASS DRAGON
WORK ORDER LGD-003
FOREST COMBAT, PROGRESSION, AND THE FIRST COMPLETE GAME DAY

STATUS

AUTHORIZED FOR IMPLEMENTATION


GOVERNING BASELINE

LGD-001 established the native Android / Jetpack Compose application and ANSI/BBS presentation shell.

LGD-002 established:

- character creation;
- persistent local GameState;
- Continue Game;
- the first real Town state;
- Character Status;
- save/version handling;
- the separation between presentation, game rules, and persistence.

LGD-003 is the first gameplay work order.

Do not redesign those foundations unless a concrete defect requires repair.


PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Turn Legend of the Glass Dragon from a persistent application shell into a genuinely playable game.

At completion, the player must be able to:

1. enter the Forest;
2. encounter monsters;
3. fight using a simple turn-based combat system;
4. win or lose;
5. earn experience and gold;
6. consume a limited number of forest fights;
7. improve through leveling/training;
8. survive or recover from defeat;
9. end the game day;
10. begin the next game day immediately;
11. repeat that loop indefinitely.

This should constitute the first complete vertical slice of the game.


PRODUCT FEEL

The experience should remain recognizably descended from an ANSI BBS door game.

Combat may look approximately like:

    -=- DEEP FOREST -=-

    A Goblin leaps from behind a fallen tree!

    Goblin
    HP: 12 / 12

    Arcturus
    HP: 20 / 20

    [1] Attack
    [2] Run Away

After an attack:

    You strike the Goblin for 5 damage!

    The Goblin claws you for 2 damage.

    Goblin HP: 7
    Your HP: 18

    [1] Attack
    [2] Run Away

The player taps these lines.

They do not type "1".

Avoid graphical health bars, character portraits, combat animations, floating numbers, card layouts, or conventional mobile-RPG battle interfaces.

The terminal text is the game.


PART 1 — DAILY FOREST FIGHTS

Establish a finite number of Forest fights per game day.

Choose one simple starting value appropriate for early testing, such as:

    10 fights per day

Centralize this value in game rules/configuration.

The Town and Forest screens must display the remaining fights clearly.

Example:

    Forest Fights Remaining: 8

Entering the Forest without actually beginning an encounter should not consume a fight.

A fight is consumed when an encounter is committed.

Do not allow the counter to become negative.


PART 2 — MONSTER CONTENT MODEL

Create a data-driven MonsterDefinition model.

At minimum support:

- stable ID;
- display name;
- minimum or intended level;
- maximum HP;
- attack;
- defense;
- experience reward;
- gold reward range or fixed reward;
- encounter text if useful.

Do not hard-code individual monster statistics inside Compose screens.

Create a small initial roster sufficient for repeated Level 1 and early-level play.

Approximately 6–10 monsters is enough.

Use light fantasy/BBS flavor.

Examples may include:

- Giant Rat;
- Cave Bat;
- Goblin;
- Highwayman;
- Wild Boar;
- Skeleton;
- Forest Spider;
- Orc Scout.

The exact list is Builder discretion.

Avoid spending this phase constructing a huge bestiary.


PART 3 — MONSTER SELECTION

When the player searches the Forest, choose an appropriate monster based primarily on player level.

The system should avoid routinely generating hopeless encounters.

A modest amount of variation is desirable.

The selection rules must live in the game layer and be testable without Compose.

If randomness is used, it should flow through the game's controlled random mechanism rather than directly calling random APIs throughout the UI.


PART 4 — COMBAT STATE

Introduce a transient or resumable combat state with enough information to represent an encounter lawfully.

At minimum:

- selected monster;
- monster current HP;
- player current HP as part of persistent player state;
- combat status;
- fight consumed;
- combat log or recent messages;
- whether escape remains available.

Combat rules must not be encoded directly in UI click handlers.

The UI should issue intents such as:

- Attack
- Run

and render the resulting state.


PART 5 — BASIC ATTACK RULE

Implement a deliberately simple combat formula.

The exact initial constants are Builder discretion, but the result should approximately reflect:

    attacker strength
    versus
    defender defense
    plus
    a small controlled random range

Damage must have a sensible minimum so fights do not deadlock through endless zero-damage turns.

Keep formulas centralized and documented.

Do not pursue final game balance in LGD-003.

The initial goal is:

- understandable;
- testable;
- tunable;
- fun enough for the first play loop.


PART 6 — TURN ORDER

For the initial implementation:

1. player chooses Attack;
2. player attack resolves;
3. if monster survives, monster attack resolves;
4. combat state updates;
5. next player choice is presented.

If the player's attack defeats the monster, it should not attack from beyond the grave.

If the monster defeats the player, no additional player action occurs.

Avoid unnecessary initiative systems at this stage.


PART 7 — VICTORY

On monster defeat:

- mark combat complete;
- grant experience;
- grant gold;
- preserve remaining player HP;
- save the updated GameState;
- show a terminal-style victory result.

Example:

    *** VICTORY! ***

    The Goblin falls!

    Experience gained: 12
    Gold found:       7

    You have 7 forest fights remaining.

    [1] Search Again
    [2] Return to Town

Search Again should begin another encounter if fights remain.

Do not force the player back through Town after every battle.


PART 8 — EXPERIENCE AND LEVEL THRESHOLDS

Implement real experience accumulation.

Create centralized level thresholds or an equivalent progression formula.

The precise balance is not final, but early levels should arrive quickly enough to exercise progression during ordinary testing.

The player must not silently jump levels solely because enough XP exists.

Use the classic RPG/BBS concept that qualifying for a level enables Training.

Example Town message:

    You feel ready for greater challenges.

    The Master at the Training Hall may test you.

This provides purpose to the Training destination.


PART 9 — TRAINING V1

Replace the Training placeholder with the first functional Training Hall.

The player may attempt training when their experience meets the requirement for the next level.

For LGD-003, keep the mechanic simple.

Acceptable implementation:

- the trainer confirms readiness;
- player chooses to undertake training;
- a short deterministic or lightly randomized training challenge resolves;
- success increases level and improves core statistics.

Alternatively, if a combat-style trainer encounter can be implemented simply using the existing combat engine, that is preferred because it reuses real gameplay.

Do not build a separate elaborate minigame.


LEVEL-UP EFFECTS

On successful training:

- increment Level by 1;
- increase maximum HP;
- increase Attack;
- increase Defense;
- restore HP either fully or according to one consistent rule;
- preserve lawful experience progression.

Centralize stat growth rules.

Display the result clearly.

Example:

    -=- TRAINING COMPLETE -=-

    You have attained Level 2!

    Maximum HP increased.
    Attack increased.
    Defense increased.

    [1] Return to Town


PART 10 — TRAINING WHEN NOT READY

If the player lacks sufficient experience, Training should say so in-world.

Example:

    The old master studies you carefully.

    "You have more to learn in the forest."

    Experience needed: 18

    [1] Return to Town

Do not use disabled modern controls as the only explanation.


PART 11 — RUNNING FROM COMBAT

Implement Run Away.

Running should:

- have a reasonable chance of success;
- resolve through game rules;
- never create an invalid combat state.

On success:

- combat ends;
- no rewards are granted;
- the already-consumed forest fight remains consumed.

On failure:

- the monster receives an attack;
- combat continues if the player survives.

Keep the rule simple and tunable.


PART 12 — PLAYER DEFEAT

Implement the first real defeat/recovery loop.

A player reduced to 0 HP should not permanently brick the save.

Use a simple BBS-style consequence.

For this phase:

- end the current combat;
- lose a meaningful but not devastating portion of carried gold;
- restore the player to a lawful recovery state;
- prevent further forest fighting during that day OR otherwise apply a clear defeat consequence;
- allow the player to proceed toward ending the day.

Example:

    -=- YOU HAVE FALLEN -=-

    Darkness takes you...

    Hours later, you awaken in the healer's care.

    Some of your gold is missing.

    Your fighting day is over.

    [1] Return to Town

Exact gold-loss percentage is Builder discretion and should be centralized.

Do not introduce permadeath.


PART 13 — HEALING

The first loop needs a practical way to recover between successful fights.

Implement a very small healing mechanism.

Preferred approach:

Add a simple Healer destination to Town.

The healer may:

- restore missing HP;
- charge a modest gold cost based on HP restored;
- refuse impossible/invalid transactions;
- optionally heal free at Level 1 if early balance proves too punishing.

Keep it simple.

Example:

    -=- THE HEALER -=-

    "I can mend those wounds."

    Health: 11 / 20
    Cost:   9 gold

    [1] Heal Me
    [2] Return to Town

Do not add consumable potion systems yet unless already trivial.


PART 14 — FIRST REAL END-OF-DAY FLOW

Replace the LGD-002 End Day placeholder.

The player must now be able to end the current game day.

Require confirmation.

Example:

    -=- END THE DAY? -=-

    You have 3 forest fights remaining.

    Once you retire for the night, these fights
    will be lost.

    [1] Sleep Until Tomorrow
    [2] Return to Town

On confirmation:

1. close any current daily activity;
2. increment the game day;
3. reset forest fights;
4. restore whatever daily flags LGD-003 uses;
5. apply the chosen overnight HP recovery rule;
6. preserve player progression, equipment, gold, and experience;
7. save the new state;
8. enter the next day's Town screen.


IMPORTANT TIME RULE

There is NO connection between game days and real-world calendar days.

The player may:

    play Day 1
    -> end the day
    -> immediately play Day 2
    -> immediately play Day 3
    -> continue as long as desired.

Do not use system date/time to enforce waiting.

Do not create cooldowns.

Do not introduce energy mechanics.

The game's day exists solely inside GameState.


PART 15 — WORLD-ADVANCEMENT HOOK

LGD-004 will introduce the simulated BBS community.

Prepare one explicit game-layer boundary for:

    advanceWorldToNextDay(...)

or equivalent.

For LGD-003 this may initially perform only player/day reset operations.

However, End Day should already route through this boundary so LGD-004 can add NPC/community simulation without rewriting the UI flow.

Do not create a speculative simulation framework beyond this small seam.


PART 16 — TOWN V1 AFTER LGD-003

Town should now reflect playable state.

At minimum show:

- current day;
- character name;
- level;
- HP;
- gold;
- forest fights remaining;
- level-up readiness when applicable.

Functional destinations:

    [1] Enter the Forest
    [2] Visit the Healer
    [3] Seek Training
    [4] Visit the Inn
    [5] View Rankings
    [6] Character
    [7] End the Day

Inn and Rankings may remain placeholders until community simulation arrives.

Character remains functional from LGD-002.


PART 17 — COMBAT TEXT PRESENTATION

Combat logs are important to the intended experience.

Use concise colored ANSI-like text.

Examples:

    You hit the Orc Scout for 6 damage!

    The Orc Scout hits you for 3 damage!

    *** CRITICAL HIT! ***

    You dodge the attack!

Do not require critical hits or dodging in this phase unless they naturally fit the simple rule system.

The examples describe presentation style, not mandatory mechanics.

The log should remain readable and not endlessly accumulate into an unusable screen.

Displaying the current exchange plus a modest recent history is sufficient.


PART 18 — SAVE DURABILITY DURING COMBAT

Avoid easy state exploits and corrupted saves.

The application should have a deliberate policy for process death during an encounter.

Preferred simple rule:

- save when an encounter begins;
- persist enough encounter state to resume the same combat after process restart;

OR, if that is materially more complex than justified:

- resolve interrupted combat through one explicit safe policy that cannot duplicate rewards or refund consumed fights.

Do not allow:

1. start fight;
2. lose badly;
3. kill application;
4. reopen with full HP and the fight restored;

unless that behavior is explicitly intended.

The Builder should document the chosen interruption policy.


PART 19 — FIRST BALANCE PASS

Perform a modest practical balance pass.

A fresh Level 1 character should ordinarily be capable of:

- winning several fights;
- being injured;
- using healing;
- earning gold;
- earning enough XP to become training-eligible within a reasonable number of fights/days;
- reaching Level 2 without extraordinary luck.

The player should also be capable of losing if careless or unlucky.

Do not attempt production-final balancing.

The purpose is to make the vertical slice enjoyable enough to evaluate.


PART 20 — LONG-RUN SAFETY

Because this game allows unlimited consecutive days, test repeated progression.

At minimum verify programmatically that:

- game day can advance repeatedly;
- daily fights reset every day;
- fights never become negative;
- HP remains within lawful bounds;
- gold does not become negative through healing/death rules;
- XP remains lawful;
- repeated save/load cycles preserve state;
- level progression remains internally consistent.

Include a lightweight simulation/test that advances at least several hundred days or repeated day transitions.

There is not yet enough autonomous world simulation to require a sophisticated soak test.


AUTOMATED TEST REQUIREMENTS

Add meaningful automated tests around the game rules.

At minimum cover:

COMBAT

- player damage;
- monster damage;
- monster defeated before retaliation;
- player defeat;
- damage cannot create invalid HP;
- reward granted exactly once;
- running success;
- running failure.

FOREST

- fight consumed at correct boundary;
- no negative fights;
- no new fight when none remain.

PROGRESSION

- XP rewards accumulate;
- training eligibility;
- successful level advancement;
- stat gains;
- illegal repeated training prevented.

HEALING

- correct restoration;
- correct gold charge;
- HP cannot exceed max HP;
- gold cannot become negative.

DAY ADVANCEMENT

- day increments exactly once;
- daily fights reset;
- progression persists;
- save/load after day transition preserves state.

DEFEAT

- recovery state is lawful;
- gold-loss rule cannot produce negative gold;
- rewards are not granted after defeat.

RANDOMNESS

Where random behavior affects tests, inject or otherwise control the random source so tests remain deterministic.


DEVICE QUALIFICATION

On the NXTPAPER tablet, if accessible, play through at least:

- character Continue;
- Town;
- multiple Forest encounters;
- victory;
- defeat or a controlled defeat test;
- healer;
- training eligibility;
- level-up;
- End Day;
- immediate next day;
- app background/resume during ordinary gameplay;
- app restart and Continue.

Specifically inspect:

- readability of combat text;
- tap targets during repeated combat;
- accidental double-tap behavior;
- scrolling;
- whether rapid taps can submit two attacks;
- whether combat remains pleasant without a keyboard.

If physical device access is unavailable, use an appropriate emulator and report the device qualification as outstanding.


ANTI-DOUBLE-ACTION REQUIREMENT

Touch interfaces introduce a risk that did not exist in the same way for typed BBS commands.

Prevent rapid repeated taps from:

- resolving two attacks for one turn;
- claiming rewards twice;
- consuming multiple fights unexpectedly;
- executing End Day twice;
- training multiple levels from one confirmation.

Game actions should be atomic from the player's perspective.


DO NOT IMPLEMENT YET

Do not implement:

- autonomous NPC adventurers;
- community rankings populated with simulated players;
- gossip/news simulation;
- PvP;
- romance/social systems;
- Inn functionality beyond placeholder content;
- shops;
- broad equipment purchasing;
- bank;
- Dragon encounters;
- multiplayer;
- networking;
- accounts;
- cloud saves;
- graphical combat;
- animation-heavy effects.

Those belong to later phases.


BUILDER DISCRETION

The Builder may select and tune:

- starting forest fights per day;
- combat formula constants;
- initial monster roster;
- XP thresholds;
- monster rewards;
- escape chance;
- death gold penalty;
- healer pricing;
- level-up stat gains;
- overnight healing rule;
- exact ANSI combat wording.

Keep all tunable values centralized wherever practical.

Favor a fun, understandable first loop over attempting to reproduce every historical LORD rule exactly.


COMPLETION CRITERIA

LGD-003 is complete when:

1. Forest is functional.
2. A data-driven monster roster exists.
3. Appropriate monsters can be encountered.
4. Forest fights are finite per game day.
5. Combat operates through touch using the ANSI menu presentation.
6. Attack resolution is functional and testable.
7. Monsters can defeat the player.
8. The player can defeat monsters.
9. Victory grants XP and gold exactly once.
10. Running from combat works.
11. HP persists between encounters.
12. Healing is available.
13. Experience produces training eligibility.
14. Training can increase the player to the next level.
15. Level-up stat growth is real and persistent.
16. Defeat has a coherent recoverable consequence.
17. End Day works.
18. Game day advances without reference to real-world time.
19. Daily forest fights reset on the next day.
20. The player may immediately continue playing consecutive days.
21. The day transition routes through a clear future world-simulation seam.
22. Save/load remains coherent through the gameplay loop.
23. Rapid touch input cannot duplicate important game actions.
24. Automated tests cover the major game-rule boundaries.
25. Repeated day advancement remains lawful over a long test run.
26. The application passes normal build/test checks.
27. NXTPAPER qualification is completed if device access exists, otherwise clearly deferred.

NEXT PLANNED WORK

LGD-004 — Simulated Adventurer Community, Rankings, Daily News, and World Advancement

That work order should introduce the defining twist of Legend of the Glass Dragon: a persistent locally simulated population of adventurers whose lives and progress advance whenever the player ends a game day.

From that point forward, beginning a new day should no longer mean merely resetting the player's fights.

It should mean discovering what happened in the realm while everyone else took their turn.
