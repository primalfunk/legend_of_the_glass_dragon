LEGEND OF THE GLASS DRAGON
WORK ORDER LGD-004
SIMULATED ADVENTURER COMMUNITY, RANKINGS, DAILY NEWS, AND WORLD ADVANCEMENT

STATUS

AUTHORIZED FOR IMPLEMENTATION


GOVERNING BASELINE

LGD-001 established:

- native Android / Kotlin / Jetpack Compose;
- ANSI/BBS presentation;
- touch-driven terminal navigation;
- local persistence.

LGD-002 established:

- persistent player identity;
- character creation;
- Continue Game;
- real GameState;
- Town and Character Status.

LGD-003 established:

- Forest combat;
- monsters;
- HP and healing;
- gold and experience;
- training and leveling;
- finite daily fights;
- player defeat/recovery;
- real game-day advancement;
- immediate consecutive-day play;
- an explicit world-advancement seam.

LGD-004 implements the feature that most distinguishes Legend of the Glass Dragon from a simple offline LORD-inspired RPG:

THE BBS COMMUNITY IS SIMULATED LOCALLY.

The player is not alone in a frozen world.

Every game day, other adventurers also take their turns.


PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Create the first persistent simulated adventurer community and integrate it into the existing game-day cycle.

At completion, ending the day must cause a population of simulated adventurers to:

- undertake plausible daily activity;
- fight;
- gain experience;
- gain levels;
- earn or lose gold;
- suffer defeats;
- recover;
- improve equipment in a lightweight manner;
- change their rankings;
- generate visible news.

The next morning, the player should immediately be able to see evidence that the realm changed.

The intended emotional result is:

"I wonder what everybody did yesterday."

not:

"The game rolled some background numbers."


CORE EXPERIENCE

A new game should create both:

- the player's character;
- a persistent local community.

The community should contain enough adventurers that rankings and daily events feel meaningful without becoming expensive or unwieldy.

A sensible initial target is approximately:

12–20 simulated adventurers.

The exact number is Builder discretion.

Their names, identities, progression, and notable history must survive save/load and day advancement.

They are persistent inhabitants of this particular game world.


IMPORTANT SIMULATION PRINCIPLE

Do not attempt to simulate every NPC battle turn-by-turn using the player combat UI engine.

The player needs detailed combat because the player experiences it.

NPC adventurers need believable outcomes.

Prefer inexpensive outcome-oriented simulation that uses the same broad progression logic without unnecessarily executing hundreds of individual combat turns.

The standard is:

BELIEVABLE CONSEQUENCES

not:

PERFECT HIDDEN MICRO-SIMULATION.


PART 1 — NPC ADVENTURER MODEL

Introduce a durable NpcAdventurer model.

At minimum support:

- stable ID;
- name;
- level;
- experience;
- maximum HP or an appropriate combat-strength representation;
- attack;
- defense;
- gold;
- weapon;
- armor;
- current status;
- personality/archetype;
- relationship or familiarity with the player;
- notable progression flags if immediately needed.

Reuse common character concepts where cleanly appropriate.

Do not force PlayerCharacter and NpcAdventurer into one overly clever inheritance hierarchy merely because they share statistics.

Prefer straightforward Kotlin data structures.


NPC STATUS

Support at least simple states such as:

- ACTIVE;
- RECOVERING.

Additional states may be introduced only if immediately useful.

A defeated NPC should not disappear permanently in this phase unless an exceptional event explicitly requires it.

This first community should remain recognizable across many days.


PART 2 — COMMUNITY CREATION

When a new game is created, generate the starting simulated population once.

Each NPC should receive:

- stable identity;
- persistent name;
- starting stats;
- modest personality variation.

Do not regenerate the community whenever the application launches.

The people encountered on Day 1 must still be the same people on Day 20 unless game events have explicitly changed that.


NPC NAMES

Provide a data-driven pool of fantasy/BBS-appropriate names.

Names should be readable, memorable, and varied.

Examples of tone:

- Raven
- Cedric
- Thorn
- Mira
- Blackwood
- Elowen
- Garrick
- Willow
- Rook
- Selene

These are examples only.

Avoid generating dozens of near-identical joke names.

A little personality and BBS-era flair is welcome.


PART 3 — NPC PERSONALITY V1

Create a very small personality system that influences daily behavior.

Do not build psychological simulation.

Approximately 4–6 archetypes are enough.

Possible examples:

AGGRESSIVE
- fights more;
- accepts more risk;
- progresses quickly but loses more often.

CAUTIOUS
- fights less;
- retreats or rests more;
- suffers fewer defeats.

AMBITIOUS
- strongly pursues experience and advancement.

GREEDY
- places greater weight on earning and keeping gold/equipment.

SOCIAL
- generates more community/gossip events.

ERRATIC
- has somewhat wider action variation.

Exact names and implementation are Builder discretion.

The important point is that two similarly leveled NPCs should not always follow identical daily behavior.


PART 4 — NPC DAILY ACTION BUDGET

Each active NPC receives a bounded daily opportunity set analogous in spirit to the player's day.

Do not literally recreate the player's interface-driven sequence.

At world advancement, determine a small number of activities for each NPC.

Potential activities:

- forest adventuring;
- resting;
- training if eligible;
- purchasing an equipment improvement;
- social/community activity;
- recovering from previous defeat.

The daily process must terminate deterministically.

No NPC may become trapped in an unbounded simulation loop.


PART 5 — NPC FOREST OUTCOMES

Implement a lightweight NPC adventuring resolver.

It should consider:

- NPC level;
- attack/defense;
- equipment;
- personality/risk tendency;
- monster difficulty or abstract encounter strength;
- controlled randomness.

Possible results:

- successful expedition;
- especially profitable expedition;
- defeat;
- uneventful/poor day.

Successful adventuring may grant:

- experience;
- gold.

Defeat may:

- reduce or remove some earned gold;
- set RECOVERING status;
- reduce the NPC's productive actions for that day.

NPC stats must remain lawful.

Do not permit negative gold, invalid HP/stat values, or impossible level states.


PART 6 — NPC LEVELING

NPCs must be able to progress through the same broad level ladder as the player.

Use the same experience thresholds unless there is a strong documented reason not to.

When an NPC becomes eligible:

- they may train;
- training may occur immediately during their simulated day or according to a simple personality-driven probability;
- successful training raises their level;
- apply equivalent or comparable stat growth to player progression.

The player should eventually see NPCs:

- remain behind;
- keep pace;
- pass them;
- become notable leaders.

Do not secretly clamp all NPCs to the player's level.


PART 7 — PROGRESSION GUARDRAILS

The community must neither:

A. remain permanently frozen behind the player;

nor:

B. rocket far beyond the player through uncontrolled compounding.

Perform a practical progression pass.

Variation is desirable.

Some NPCs should perform better than others.

The leading adventurer should be able to change over time.

Do not hard-script the player to always be special or always be rank #1.


PART 8 — EQUIPMENT PROGRESSION V1

Introduce lightweight NPC equipment improvement.

Do not build the full player shop yet.

Use a small data-driven equipment ladder compatible with the existing attack/defense concepts.

NPCs with sufficient gold may occasionally upgrade:

- weapon;
- armor.

Equipment decisions may depend on:

- affordability;
- current gear;
- personality.

Their gold should be reduced appropriately.

This produces visible community stories such as:

    Raven bought a Steel Blade.

or:

    Cedric finally replaced his battered armor.

The player does not yet need equivalent shopping functionality in this work order unless an existing LGD-003 implementation already supports it cleanly.


PART 9 — RANKINGS

Replace the Rankings placeholder with a functional persistent community leaderboard.

The rankings should include:

- the player;
- all active NPC adventurers.

Primary ordering should reflect:

1. level;
2. experience or equivalent progress toward next level;
3. a stable tie-breaker.

Example:

    -=- HEROES OF THE REALM -=-

     1. Raven........ Level 4
     2. Arcturus..... Level 3   < YOU
     3. Mira......... Level 3
     4. Thorn........ Level 2
     5. Cedric....... Level 2

    [1] Return to Town

The exact formatting may adapt to screen width.

Do not use a modern table/grid component visually.

Rankings should look like a BBS text listing.


PLAYER POSITION

If the list becomes longer than the visible screen:

- allow touch scrolling;
- clearly mark the player;
- do not sacrifice readability to force everything onto one page.


PART 10 — DAILY NEWS / REALM NEWS

Create a persistent daily event/news system.

World advancement should produce a bounded set of notable events.

Examples:

    -=- YESTERDAY IN THE REALM -=-

    Raven reached Level 3!

    Mira was carried from the forest after
    tangling with an Ogre.

    Cedric purchased a new Iron Sword.

    Thorn overtook Arcturus in the rankings.

    Selene returned to adventuring after
    recovering from her wounds.

The exact tone should remain playful and BBS-like.


WORLD EVENT MODEL

Introduce a durable WorldEvent or equivalent containing at minimum:

- stable/event ID if useful;
- game day;
- event type;
- involved character IDs;
- human-readable presentation data or sufficient structured data to produce it;
- importance/notability if needed.

Prefer structured events plus presentation formatting over permanently storing arbitrary UI strings when practical.

However, do not build an elaborate localization architecture for this phase.


PART 11 — NEWS CURATION

Do not dump every hidden NPC action into the news feed.

If 16 adventurers each perform several actions, the player does not need 50 lines of bookkeeping every morning.

Generate or select a modest number of interesting events.

Prioritize:

- level gains;
- defeats;
- recoveries;
- major gold/equipment changes;
- rank changes;
- unusual successful days;
- social/gossip events.

Routine uneventful actions may remain invisible.

A typical new day should produce roughly 3–8 visible items when enough meaningful events occurred.

It is acceptable for a quiet day to produce fewer.


PART 12 — MORNING REPORT

After End Day advances the world, do not immediately drop the player into Town without context.

Introduce a brief new-day report.

Example:

    +----------------------------------+
    |            DAY 7                 |
    +----------------------------------+

    You awaken to another day in the realm.

    While you slept...

    Raven reached Level 4.
    Mira was defeated in the forest.
    Thorn purchased a new blade.

    [1] Enter the Village Square
    [2] Read All News

This is a major part of the game's community illusion.

The player should immediately understand:

THE WORLD JUST TOOK ITS TURN.


PART 13 — NEWS ARCHIVE

Make recent realm news accessible from Town or Inn.

A simple initial implementation is sufficient.

Support viewing at least several recent game days.

Example:

    -=- REALM NEWS -=-

    Day 8
      Raven reached Level 4.
      Cedric was defeated by a Troll.

    Day 7
      Mira purchased Chain Armor.
      Selene passed Thorn in the rankings.

    [1] Older News
    [2] Return to Town

Touch scrolling may replace explicit paging if that fits the established terminal style.

Bound retained news history sensibly if unlimited storage is unnecessary.

Do not silently let textual logs grow without limit for tens of thousands of game days.


PART 14 — INN V1

Replace the Inn placeholder with the first functional community-facing location.

Keep its scope modest.

For LGD-004, the Inn should principally provide access to community information.

Suggested menu:

    -=- THE DRAGON'S REST -=-

    The room is thick with smoke, ale,
    and exaggerated stories.

    [1] Hear the Latest Gossip
    [2] Read Realm News
    [3] See Who Is Here
    [4] Return to Town

"Who Is Here" may show a small selection of simulated adventurers for the current day.

Do not implement elaborate conversation trees yet.


PART 15 — GOSSIP V1

Create lightweight gossip based on actual community state where possible.

Examples:

    "I heard Raven thinks he's the strongest
    fighter in town."

    "Mira has been spending a great deal of
    gold at the armorer lately."

    "Thorn hasn't been the same since that
    beating in the forest."

Prefer gossip that references:

- real NPCs;
- actual levels;
- rank position;
- recent events;
- equipment;
- player standing.

A small number of generic filler lines may exist, but the system should not merely choose disconnected random flavor text.


PART 16 — PLAYER FAMILIARITY V1

Introduce one minimal relationship/familiarity value between the player and each NPC.

Do not implement romance or a detailed relationship system yet.

For this phase, familiarity may change when:

- the NPC appears in an Inn encounter;
- the player reads/interacts with a small community event;
- future systems explicitly reference the NPC.

The value need not yet produce many effects.

The purpose is to establish durable groundwork for later rivalry/friendship mechanics without overbuilding them.


PART 17 — RANK CHANGE EVENTS

Track meaningful changes in standing.

When appropriate, generate news for events such as:

    Raven has taken the #1 position.

    You have passed Cedric in the rankings.

    Mira has overtaken you.

Do not produce spam for insignificant tie-break reshuffling.

The player should begin to recognize particular rivals naturally.


PART 18 — WORLD ADVANCEMENT ORDER

LGD-003 created the world-advancement seam.

LGD-004 must make its ordering explicit.

A sensible transition is:

1. confirm player ends the day;
2. finalize player daily state;
3. snapshot relevant prior rankings/state;
4. advance recovering NPCs;
5. resolve each active NPC's daily actions;
6. resolve NPC training/equipment effects;
7. recompute rankings;
8. derive notable rank changes;
9. create daily WorldEvents/news;
10. increment or establish the next game day consistently;
11. reset player's daily allowances;
12. apply overnight player recovery;
13. persist the complete resulting GameState atomically;
14. display the morning report.

The exact implementation order may vary if current GameState semantics increment the day at a different boundary.

What matters is that:

- each world day resolves exactly once;
- results are coherent;
- the player cannot accidentally trigger the same NPC day twice.


PART 19 — ATOMIC DAY TRANSITION

End Day is now a high-value state transition.

Treat it as one logical operation.

Rapid tapping, activity recreation, or navigation must not:

- advance NPCs twice;
- increment the day twice;
- duplicate events;
- award NPC rewards twice;
- reset the player twice.

Persist the final result coherently.

If the application is interrupted during advancement, it must reopen into a lawful state without inventing a second world day.

Use the simplest robust strategy appropriate to the existing persistence architecture.


PART 20 — DETERMINISTIC RANDOMNESS

The community simulation needs substantial random variation.

Keep it controllable.

Tests must be able to run world advancement with deterministic random input or fixed seeds.

Where practical, a saved world seed / PRNG state should ensure a given transition cannot produce one result, crash midway, and then produce a completely different duplicate outcome after recovery.

Do not call uncontrolled global randomness from many unrelated classes.


PART 21 — SIMULATION PERFORMANCE

The initial community is intentionally small.

World advancement on the NXTPAPER should feel effectively immediate.

Do not:

- create one coroutine per NPC unnecessarily;
- run heavyweight AI;
- simulate hidden frame-by-frame combat;
- perform network activity;
- write dozens of persistence transactions during one End Day.

Resolve the day's simulation in memory and commit a coherent resulting state.


PART 22 — COMMUNITY INSPECTION

Allow the player to inspect another adventurer from Rankings or the Inn.

A simple profile is sufficient.

Example:

    -=- RAVEN -=-

    Level....... 4
    Rank........ 1
    Weapon...... Steel Sword
    Armor....... Chain Mail
    Status...... Adventuring

    "Raven has become one of the most
     feared fighters in the realm."

    [1] Return

Do not expose hidden personality coefficients or simulation internals.

Display only information a BBS player might plausibly know.


PART 23 — INITIAL COMMUNITY BALANCE

Run the community for enough simulated days to determine whether it feels alive.

Expected behavior across roughly 30–100 simulated days:

- multiple NPCs gain levels;
- the ranking order changes;
- some NPCs perform considerably better than others;
- defeats occur;
- equipment upgrades occur;
- nobody routinely acquires impossible values;
- no single personality type automatically dominates every world;
- NPCs do not all advance in lockstep.

The exact outcome is not prescribed.

Uniformity is undesirable.


PART 24 — LONG-RUN SIMULATION QUALIFICATION

This work order introduces the first meaningful autonomous system.

Add a deterministic simulation soak test.

Advance a world for at least:

    1,000 game days

without Compose/UI interaction.

Verify continuously or at the end:

- day count is correct;
- all stable NPC IDs remain unique;
- NPC names/identities persist;
- levels remain lawful;
- experience remains lawful;
- gold never becomes negative;
- rankings contain each eligible character exactly once;
- the player remains represented correctly;
- NPC recovery never becomes permanently stuck;
- event generation remains bounded;
- news retention remains bounded if a limit is used;
- no simulation action creates NaN/overflow/invalid numeric states;
- day advancement always terminates.

Do not tune the entire game's future progression curve around what happens after Day 1,000.

The soak test is for structural stability.


PART 25 — PLAYER INACTIVITY IS NOT REAL TIME

Reinforce the project rule:

NPCs advance only when the player advances the GAME DAY.

They do not advance merely because:

- the app was closed overnight;
- the tablet clock changed;
- a week passed in real life;
- the application resumed from background.

If the player closes the application on Day 8 and returns a month later, the game is still on Day 8.

The world advances when the player chooses End Day.


PART 26 — SAVE COMPATIBILITY

LGD-004 changes the GameState schema materially.

Handle existing LGD-003 saves deliberately.

Acceptable strategies:

A. migrate a lawful LGD-003 save by creating the NPC community and related state;

or

B. if pre-release development policy permits save reset, detect the prior schema and provide an explicit development-era reset path.

Do not crash.

Do not silently reinterpret incompatible data.

Document the chosen approach.


PART 27 — TEST REQUIREMENTS

Add meaningful automated tests for:

COMMUNITY CREATION

- expected population size;
- unique stable IDs;
- legal names/stats;
- personality assignment;
- persistence round trip.

DAILY SIMULATION

- each NPC advances at most once per day;
- bounded daily actions;
- successful adventuring;
- defeat;
- recovery;
- XP and gold rewards;
- lawful progression.

NPC LEVELING

- threshold behavior;
- level increment;
- stat growth;
- no repeated illegal level-up.

EQUIPMENT

- affordability;
- upgrade replaces weaker item;
- purchase cost applied exactly once;
- gold never becomes negative.

RANKINGS

- player included;
- all eligible NPCs included exactly once;
- correct primary ordering;
- stable tie-break behavior;
- rank changes detected correctly.

NEWS

- notable events generated from real changes;
- uninteresting action volume does not flood the feed;
- event day is correct;
- retention limit works if implemented.

DAY ADVANCEMENT

- world advances exactly once;
- rapid/repeated End Day submission cannot duplicate advancement;
- player daily state resets correctly;
- NPC simulation and player reset occur in one coherent transition.

LONG RUN

- deterministic 1,000-day simulation remains lawful.


PART 28 — DEVICE QUALIFICATION

If NXTPAPER device access exists, qualify:

- End Day;
- morning report;
- Rankings;
- scrolling through rankings;
- inspecting NPCs;
- Inn;
- gossip;
- recent news;
- several consecutive game days;
- ranking movement;
- NPC level-up event;
- NPC defeat/recovery event;
- background/resume around the morning report;
- app restart and Continue after world advancement.

Pay particular attention to text density.

The richer community presentation must still feel comfortable on a touchscreen terminal.

If physical access is unavailable, perform emulator qualification and clearly report the device test as outstanding.


VISUAL DIRECTION

Do not turn community information into:

- avatar cards;
- profile pictures;
- progress rings;
- graphical leaderboard podiums;
- chat bubbles;
- social-media feeds.

This remains a BBS.

Prefer screens like:

    -=- WHO'S ONLINE? -=-

    Raven.......... Level 4
    Mira........... Level 3
    Thorn.......... Level 3

and:

    -=- REALM NEWS -=-

    * Raven reached Level 4!
    * Mira was defeated in the forest.
    * Thorn bought a new sword.

The simulation may be modern.

Its presentation should remain antique.


DO NOT IMPLEMENT YET

Do not implement:

- player-vs-NPC combat;
- killing other players;
- NPC permadeath;
- romance;
- marriage;
- complex friendship trees;
- clans/guilds;
- mail;
- multiplayer;
- online chat;
- networking;
- cloud simulation;
- real-time NPC activity;
- extensive shops;
- bank;
- Glass Dragon attempts;
- generative AI dialogue.

Those belong to later development.


BUILDER DISCRETION

The Builder may choose:

- exact NPC population size;
- personality archetypes;
- daily NPC action counts;
- NPC outcome formulas;
- progression-rate tuning;
- equipment ladder details;
- news retention size;
- ranking tie-break rules;
- exact gossip wording;
- how many NPCs appear "at the Inn" on a given day;
- exact morning-report layout.

Favor:

- simple;
- legible;
- persistent;
- deterministic enough to test;
- varied enough to surprise.


COMPLETION CRITERIA

LGD-004 is complete when:

1. Every new game contains a persistent simulated adventurer population.
2. NPC identities survive save/load.
3. NPCs have modest behavioral/personality variation.
4. Ending the day advances the NPC community.
5. NPCs can adventure successfully.
6. NPCs can be defeated.
7. Defeated NPCs can recover.
8. NPCs gain experience and gold.
9. NPCs can gain levels.
10. NPCs can improve equipment.
11. NPC progression does not simply mirror the player's progress.
12. Rankings contain the player and NPC population.
13. Rankings change as the world develops.
14. The player can inspect other adventurers.
15. World advancement produces structured notable events.
16. Daily news exposes meaningful NPC activity.
17. The player receives a morning report after world advancement.
18. Recent news can be revisited.
19. The Inn provides the first functional community-facing experience.
20. Gossip references real community state where practical.
21. The player's familiarity with NPCs has a durable minimal foundation.
22. Rank changes can become visible community events.
23. A world day cannot be advanced twice accidentally.
24. NPC activity is driven solely by game-day advancement, never wall-clock time.
25. World simulation uses controlled/testable randomness.
26. Community advancement performs efficiently on the target device.
27. A deterministic 1,000-day simulation remains structurally lawful.
28. Schema compatibility with earlier development saves is handled deliberately.
29. Normal build/test checks pass.
30. NXTPAPER qualification is completed if device access exists, otherwise explicitly deferred.
